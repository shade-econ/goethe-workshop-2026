## Slide 4: Representative agent NK model

- Separable preferences, sticky prices or wages, linearized + perfect foresight:

- Aggregate demand: Euler equation

$$
c_t = c_{t+1} - \frac{1}{\sigma}(i_t - \pi_{t+1})
$$

- Inflation: New Keynesian Phillips curve

$$
\pi_t = \kappa c_t + \beta \pi_{t+1}
$$

- Monetary policy: real interest rate rule

$$
i_t = \pi_{t+1} + \epsilon_t
$$

Can also use more standard Taylor rule:

$$
i_t = \phi \pi_t + \epsilon_t
$$

Image note: Arrow points from the real-rate rule to the alternative Taylor rule.

## Slide 5: Monetary transmission in RANK

Monetary accommodation

$$
\epsilon_t \downarrow
$$

Monetary rule:

$$
i_t - \pi_{t+1} = \epsilon_t
$$

Real rate $\downarrow$

Euler equation + $c_\infty = 0$:

$$
c_t = c_{t+1} - \frac{1}{\sigma}(i_t - \pi_{t+1})
$$

Aggregate demand $\uparrow$

Phillips curve:

$$
\pi_t = \kappa c_t + \beta \pi_{t+1}
$$

Inflation $\uparrow$

Feedback with standard Taylor rule:

$$
i_t = \phi \pi_t + \epsilon_t
$$

HANK revisits this link! Two key questions:

1. Is transmission through Euler equation alone plausible?
2. Is aggregate effect plausible?

## Slide 9: Review: canonical HANK model

- Household $i \in [0,1]$ solves:

$$
\max_{c_{it}} \mathbb{E}_0 \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \beta_{it}\left(u(c_{it}) - v(N_t)\right)
$$

subject to

$$
c_{it} + a_{it} \leq (1 + r_t^p)a_{it-1} + Z_t e_{it}
$$

$$
a_{it} \geq 0
$$

Given

$$
\{r_t^p, Z_t\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
$$

we obtain

$$
\{C_t, A_t\}_{t=0}^{\infty}.
$$

- Given $e_{it}$, $r_{ss}$, $Z_{ss}$, we calibrated $\beta_{it}$ to match aggregate $A_{ss}$ and MPCs
  - Pick calibration with $Z/Y = 0.7$ and $B/Y = 1$ annually

- Assume no markups $\mu = 1$, so all assets are real government bonds, $A_{ss} = B_{ss}$

- Q: What happens after a monetary policy shock?

## Slide 10: Interest rate changes: timing and fiscal consequences

- Monetary policy rule:

$$
i_t = \pi_{t+1} + \epsilon_t
$$

- Real interest rate between $t$ and $t+1$ is $\epsilon_t$, for all $t \geq 0$:

$$
r^p_{t+1} = \epsilon_t \equiv r_t
$$

“Ex-ante” $r$

- Real interest rate between $t=-1$ and $t=0$ is $r_{ss}$!

$$
r^p_0 = r_{ss}
$$

“Ex-post” $r$

- Government budget constraint with this notation:

$$
B_t = (1 + r_{t-1})B_{t-1} + G_t - T_t
$$

- Fiscal rule: keep $G_t = G_{ss}$, adjust $T_t$ to keep

$$
(1 + r_t)B_t = (1 + r_{ss})B_{ss}
$$

constant.

Image note: Right-side annotation distinguishes the ex-ante real rate $r_t$ from the ex-post real rate $r^p_0$ using arrows and underlines.

## Slide 11: IKC revisited: monetary policy

- Model in sequence space:

$$
Y_t = G_{ss} + \mathcal{C}_t\left(\{r_s, Y_s - T_s\}\right)
$$

$$
T_t = T_{ss} + \frac{r_t - r_{ss}}{1 + r_t}B_{ss}
$$

- Linearize:

$$
d\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{M}^r d\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{M} d\mathbf{Y} - \mathbf{M} d\mathbf{T}
$$

$$
d\mathbf{T} = B_{ss}d\mathbf{r}
$$

- New sequence space Jacobian:

$$
M^r_{t,s} \equiv \frac{\partial \mathcal{C}_t}{\partial \log(1 + r_s)},
\qquad
d\mathbf{r} \equiv \frac{1}{1+r}(dr_0, dr_1, \ldots)
$$

Image note: The linearized equation $d\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{M}^r d\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{M} d\mathbf{Y} - \mathbf{M} d\mathbf{T}$ is boxed in red.

## Slide 13: Solving the IKC with monetary shocks

- Monetary policy shocks:

$$
d\mathbf{Y} = (\mathbf{M}^r - \mathbf{M} B_{ss})d\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{M}d\mathbf{Y}
$$

- Fiscal policy shocks:

$$
d\mathbf{Y} = d\mathbf{G} - \mathbf{M}d\mathbf{T} + \mathbf{M}d\mathbf{Y}
$$

- Same logic: zero NPV impulse (why?)

$$
\mathbf{q}'(\mathbf{M}^r - \mathbf{M} B_{ss}) = 0
$$

- Same approach to solving:
  - in goods space, using $\mathcal{M}$ from lec 2:

$$
d\mathbf{Y} = \mathcal{M}(\mathbf{M}^r - \mathbf{M} B_{ss})d\mathbf{r}
$$

  - in asset space, linearizing asset market clearing:

$$
\mathcal{A}_t\left(\{r_s, Y_s - T_s\}\right) = B_t
$$

$$
T_t = T_{ss} + \frac{r_t - r_{ss}}{1 + r_t}B_{ss}
$$

$$
B_t = \frac{1 + r_{ss}}{1 + r_t}B_{ss}
$$

$$
d\mathbf{Y} = -\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{A}_r d\mathbf{r} + (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}^{-1})B_{ss}d\mathbf{r}
$$

## Slide 18: Zero liquidity limit

- One way to get analytical traction: take $B_{ss} \to 0$

- Since hh. borrowing limit is zero, this leads us to the “zero liquidity limit”:
  - Since no one borrows, in equilibrium also no one lends
  - So, in equilibrium, everyone has $c_{it} = z_{it}$

- How do we get there? keep $r$ constant but recalibrate $\beta$ until we hit $B_{ss} \simeq 0$

## Slide 20: Interpretation and importance of GE

- Agent with highest motive to save is on their Euler equation:

$$
c_{it}^{-1/\sigma}
=
\beta_{it}(1+r_t)\mathbb{E}\left[c_{it+1}^{-1/\sigma}\right]
$$

- In eqbm $c_{it} = z_{it}$ in all states, but also $z_{it} = e_{it}Z_t$ and $Z_t = Y_t - T_t = C_t$. So:

$$
C_t^{-1/\sigma}
=
(1+r_t)\beta_{it}
\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[e_{it+1}^{-1/\sigma}\right]}{e_{it}^{-1/\sigma}}
C_{t+1}^{-1/\sigma}
=
\tilde{\beta}(1+r_t)C_{t+1}^{-1/\sigma}
$$

$$
\tilde{\beta}
\equiv
\max_i
\beta_{it}
\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[e_{it+1}^{-1/\sigma}\right]}{e_{it}^{-1/\sigma}}
$$

- Equilibrium $C$ follows Euler equation too, despite EE failing for most agents!

- Idea: unc’d agents respond to $r$, other agents respond to $Y \to$ amplification!

- Werning (2015) equivalence result, version 1 (zero liquidity, any $\sigma$)
