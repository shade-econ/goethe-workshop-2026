## Slide 2: Calculating sequence-space Jacobians

## Slide 3: What are sequence-space Jacobians?

* Take some function mapping sequences of aggregate input to aggregate output
* Examples so far: consumption and assets as functions of after-tax income $Z_t$:

$$
C_t = \mathcal{C}_t\left(\{Z_s\}_{s=0}^{\infty}\right)
\quad \text{and} \quad
A_t = \mathcal{A}_t\left(\{Z_s\}_{s=0}^{\infty}\right)
$$

* **Sequence-space Jacobians** are the derivatives of these functions:

  * Usually around the steady state
  * In principle, dimensions $\infty \times \infty$, though usually truncate to finite $T \times T$
  * Can have many, mapping multiple inputs to multiple outputs
* Example: Jacobian of $\mathcal{C}$ is matrix $\mathbf{M}$ of “intertemporal MPCs”
* As we saw in fiscal lecture, can use to easily solve for impulse responses

## Slide 5: How do we calculate sequence-space Jacobians?

* Sequence-space Jacobians are awesome if we have them
* But how do we get them in the first place for a heterogeneous-agent model?
* Each column is an impulse response to perturbation only at $s$…
* Do we need to redo this process $T$ times, once for each $s$, at $O(NT^2)$ cost?

$$
\mu_0 = \mu_{ss}
$$

$$
V_T = V_{ss}
$$

[Where “$N$” is dimensionality of het-agent model.]

**Image note:** Diagram of repeated backward and forward arrows across time, with ellipses in the middle, illustrating the brute-force process of separately recomputing impulse responses for each shock date $s$.

## Slide 6: We can do better

* The “direct” or **“brute-force”** method is costly:

  * if $N \gg T$, then $O(NT^2)$ work to get Jacobians swamps $O(T^3)$ cost of matrix operations
  * (still not totally useless, especially if we can reuse them)
* Fortunately, there’s a better way: the **“fake news algorithm”**

  * Need (roughly) *single* backward and forward pass, not one for each $s$
  * Reduces bottleneck steps to $O(NT)$
  * Reference: SSJ paper (Auclert, Bardóczy, Rognlie, Straub, Econometrica 2021)

## Slide 7: General setup (similar to SSJ paper notation)

* Let superscript $s$ denote infinitesimal shock $dZ_s = dx$ at date $s$

  * Income at all other dates remains in steady state
* Iterate backward to get policy functions $\mathbf{c}_t^s$ and transition matrices over discretized states $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_t^s$ at each date, represented respectively as a length-$N$ vector and an $N \times N$ matrix.
* Distribution (over discretized states) and aggregate consumption given by

$$
\mathbf{D}_{t+1}^s = \left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_t^s\right)^\prime \mathbf{D}_t^s
$$

$$
C_t^s = \left(\mathbf{c}_t^s\right)^\prime \mathbf{D}_t^s
$$

* Then calculate intertemporal MPCs $M_{t,s} = \partial C_t / \partial Z_s$ as $dC_t^s / dx$

## Slide 8: Insight: only need to iterate backward once!

* Iterate backward separately to recalculate $\mathbf{c}_t^s$ and $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_t^s$ for each $s$? **No!**
* Why? Because **only distance to the shock matters** for policy function:

$$
\mathbf{c}_t^s = \mathbf{c}_{t+h}^{s+h}
\quad \text{for any } h
$$

* So, just consider one shock at maximal horizon $s = T - 1$, then write (same for $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$)

$$
\mathbf{c}_t^s =
\begin{cases}
\mathbf{c}_{ss}, & s < t, \\
\mathbf{c}_{T-1-(s-t)}^{T-1}, & s \ge t.
\end{cases}
$$

## Slide 9: Very helpful, but still lots of work

* Backward iteration often costliest, so this is a big help!
* But still, for each $s$, need to iterate forward on distribution
* Economized on top steps but not bottom:

$$
\mathbf{D}_0 = \mathbf{D}_{ss}
$$

$$
V_T = V_{ss}
$$

**Image note:** Diagram again shows backward arrows on top and forward arrows on bottom across a time sequence with ellipses; the point is that backward steps have been economized, but forward distribution iteration remains for each $s$.

## Slide 10: What’s going on?

* We care about aggregate

$$
C_t^s = \left(\mathbf{c}_t^s\right)^\prime \mathbf{D}_t^s
$$

[or, more specifically, $M_{t,s} \equiv dC_t^s / dx$]

* We have $\mathbf{c}_t^s = \mathbf{c}_{t+h}^{s+h}$, but that’s not true for $\mathbf{D}_t^s$: generally $\mathbf{D}_t^s \ne \mathbf{D}_{t+h}^{s+h}$
* Theorem: to first order,

$$
d\mathbf{D}_t^s - d\mathbf{D}_{t-1}^{s-1}
=
\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ss}^\prime\right)^{t-1} d\mathbf{D}_1^s.
$$

* Why? If shock happens at $s$ instead of $s - 1$, **one more period to anticipate it**

  * $\to$ affects date 0 policy $\to$ affects date-1 distribution $d\mathbf{D}_1^s$
  * $\to$ carries over to date $t$ distribution via $t - 1$ applications of $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ss}^\prime$

## Slide 11: Effect on aggregates

* We have

$$
d\mathbf{D}_t^s - d\mathbf{D}_{t-1}^{s-1}
=
\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ss}^\prime\right)^{t-1} d\mathbf{D}_1^s.
$$

* Effect on $dC_t^s - dC_{t-1}^{s-1} = \mathbf{c}_{ss}^\prime \left(d\mathbf{D}_t^s - d\mathbf{D}_{t-1}^{s-1}\right)$ is therefore:

$$
\mathbf{c}_{ss}^\prime
\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ss}^\prime\right)^{t-1}
d\mathbf{D}_1^s
\quad
\left(\equiv F_{t,s} \cdot dx\right)
$$

* The matrix $F_{t,s}$ is closely related to Jacobian $M_{t,s}$ via

$$
F_{t,s} = M_{t,s} - M_{t-1,s-1}
$$

* Can reconstruct $M_{t,s}$ from diagonals $F_{t,s}$, defining $F_{t,s} \equiv M_{t,s}$ for $t$ or $s = 0$:

$$
M_{3,4} = F_{3,4} + F_{2,3} + F_{1,2} + F_{0,1}
$$

## Slide 12: What is this F (“fake news matrix”)?

* For $t, s > 0$, we have

$$
F_{t,s} = M_{t,s} - M_{t-1,s-1}
$$

* Why are $M_{t,s}$ and $M_{t-1,s-1}$ different?

  * Because former has **one extra period of anticipation**
  * $F_{t,s}$ is the effect at $t$ of having thought, at 0, that there would be shock at $s$
* One interpretation: “fake news shock”

  * $F_{\cdot,s}$ is impulse response to shock at $s$ announced at 0, rescinded at 1

## Slide 16: Where we stand now

* Reduced finding Jacobian $\mathbf{M}$ to “fake news matrix” $\mathbf{F}$
* Simple formula for $F_{t,s}$ when $t > 0$:

$$
F_{t,s} dx
=
\mathbf{c}_{ss}^\prime
\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ss}^\prime\right)^{t-1}
d\mathbf{D}_1^s
$$

* Problem: still seems like a lot of work to apply $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ss}^\prime$ repeatedly to each $d\mathbf{D}_1^s$!
* Solution: evaluate formula from the **left**, not the **right**!
* Calculate “expectation functions” $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_t \equiv \left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ss}\right)^t \mathbf{c}_{ss}$ only once, then evaluate $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_t^\prime d\mathbf{D}_1^s$

  * $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_t$ is expected $c$ in $t$ periods for a household who follows steady-state policy

## Slide 17: Cracked it open, now have four-step algorithm

* **Step 1:** iterate backward once from shock $dZ_{T-1} = dx$ to obtain all $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_t^s$, $\mathbf{c}_t^s$

  * define $\mathcal{Y}_s dx \equiv \left(d\mathbf{c}_0^s\right)^\prime \mathbf{D}_{ss}$ and $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_s dx \equiv \left(d\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_0^s\right)^\prime \mathbf{D}_{ss}$
* **Step 2:** repeatedly apply $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ss}$ to calculate expectation functions $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_t \equiv \left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{ss}\right)^t \mathbf{c}_{ss}$
* **Step 3:** form fake news matrix, which is $F_{0,s} = \mathcal{Y}_s$ and $F_{t,s} = \boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_{t-1}^\prime \boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_s$ ($t > 0$)
* **Step 4:** calculate all $M_{t,s}$ by cumulatively summing diagonals of $F_{t,s}$
* First 2 steps are $O(NT)$, step 3 is $O(NT^2)$ but can be written as giant matrix multiplication (super efficient, never the bottleneck), step 4 is $O(T^2)$

## Slide 18: Summary: the “fake news algorithm”

* Most complex of ideas so far, but now sequence-space Jacobians are practical!

  * Key step is only $O(NT)$, far better than the $O(N^3)$ of state-space methods
  * Example was iMPCs $\mathbf{M}$, but same method for any other Jacobian
  * Various implementation details (for multiple inputs / outputs, numerical vs. automatic differentiation, …): see SSJ paper and appendix
* Reducing Jacobians to “fake news matrices” an interesting step in own right

  * Isolate effects of information, useful for deviations from FIRE

## Slide 20: Linear and nonlinear systems

* Asset version of the Intertemporal Keynesian Cross model (last lecture):

$$
\mathcal{A}_{t}\left(\left\{ {Y_{s}}-T_{s}\right\}_{s=0}^\infty  \right)=B_{t}
$$

* Solve **linearized** case with simple matrix operations, where $\mathbf{A}$ is SSJ of $\mathcal{A}$:

$$
\mathbf{A}(d\mathbf{Y} - d\mathbf{T}) = d\mathbf{B}
\quad \Rightarrow \quad
d\mathbf{Y} = d\mathbf{T} + \mathbf{A}^{-1} d\mathbf{B}
$$

* For a **nonlinear** impulse response, solve using version of Newton’s method, using $\mathbf{A}$ as approximate Jacobian even around nonlinear path
* Iterate over guesses $\mathbf{Y}^{(j)}$ until asset market clearing approximately holds:

$$
\mathbf{Y}^{(j)}
=
\mathbf{Y}^{(j-1)}
-
\mathbf{A}^{-1}
\left(
\mathcal{A}\left(\mathbf{Y}^{(j-1)} - \mathbf{T}\right) - \mathbf{B}
\right).
$$

## Slide 22: Advantages of the sequence space and Jacobians

## Slide 23: Any shock, any heterogeneity

* For linear case, we solved

$$
d\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{A}^{-1} d\mathbf{B} + d\mathbf{T}
$$

* Once we’ve calculated inverse asset Jacobian $\mathbf{A}^{-1}$, we can solve for response to any time path of $d\mathbf{B}$ and $d\mathbf{T}$ almost instantly
* Similar “general equilibrium Jacobian” mapping in more complex cases
* Suppose we want 100 different types, over and above our heterogeneity

  * Just take weighted average of the $\mathbf{A}$s to get economy-wide $\mathbf{A}$

## Slide 24: Some advantages of the sequence space: summary

1. Can get response to any **shock**
2. Can easily handle almost any **heterogeneity**
3. Can simulate, get any **second moments**, even **estimate model** [to come!]
4. Can implement **non-rational expectations** [to come!]
5. Can get **informative decompositions** [e.g. $d\mathbf{Y} = d\mathbf{G} - \mathbf{M} d\mathbf{T} + \mathbf{M} d\mathbf{Y}$]

(These advantages carry over in part to any MIT shock / sequence-space method, but best by far when we’re using Jacobians!)
