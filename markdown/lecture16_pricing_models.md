## Slide 2: The New-Keynesian Phillips Curve

$$
\pi_t = \kappa \widehat{mc}_t + \beta \mathbb{E}_t \pi_{t+1}
$$

- Built on strong assumptions:
  - Rotemberg or Calvo pricing
  - Not in line with micro data!
- Not in line with macro data either!
  - no inertia, too forward looking
  - slope $\kappa$ too high!

## Slide 5: Warm-Up: What is a “Phillips curve”?

$$
\{\widehat{mc}_t\} \longrightarrow \{\pi_t\}
$$

$$
\{\widehat{MC}_t\} \longrightarrow \{\widehat{P}_t\} \Longleftrightarrow \{\pi_t\}
$$

This is the heart of the Phillips curve!

$$
\widehat{MC}_t = \widehat{mc}_t + \widehat{P}_t
$$

Image note: Flow diagram links marginal cost sequences to inflation. A large orange arrow connects $\{\widehat{mc}_t\}$ to $\{\pi_t\}$, and curved arrows connect $\{\widehat{MC}_t\}$ and $\{\widehat{P}_t\}$, emphasizing the price-level pass-through step.

## Slide 6: Two crucial Jacobians

- Pass-Through Matrix (this is what is at the heart of the Phillips curve)

$$
\widehat{\mathbf{P}} = \Psi \cdot \widehat{\mathbf{MC}}
$$

- Generalized Phillips Curve

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} = \mathbf{K} \cdot \widehat{\mathbf{mc}}
$$

- We can derive $\mathbf{K}$ from $\Psi$ …

$$
\mathbf{K} = (\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L})(\mathbf{I}-\Psi)^{-1}\Psi
$$

## Slide 7: Calvo model

$$
\Psi \equiv (1-\theta)
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\
\theta & 1 & 0 & \cdots \\
\theta^2 & \theta & 1 & \cdots \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}
\cdot
(1-\beta\theta)
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & \beta\theta & (\beta\theta)^2 & \cdots \\
0 & 1 & \beta\theta & \cdots \\
0 & 0 & 1 & \cdots \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\mathbf{K} =
\begin{pmatrix}
\kappa & \beta\kappa & \beta^2\kappa & \cdots \\
0 & \kappa & \beta\kappa & \cdots \\
0 & 0 & \kappa & \cdots \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
\kappa = \frac{(1-\theta)(1-\beta\theta)}{\theta}
\Longleftrightarrow
\pi_t = \kappa \widehat{mc}_t + \beta \mathbb{E}_t \pi_{t+1}
$$

Special case of a “time dependent” model with exponential “survival function” $\theta^t$.

## Slide 10: Menu cost model

- Mass 1 of firms, choosing distance to their optimal price (“price gap”) $x_{it}$
- Without any shocks, want to set $x_{it}=0$! With shocks, want $x_{it}=\widehat{MC}_t$
- But each price reset costs some $\xi > 0$ (“menu cost”)
- If price is not reset, price gap moves: $x_{it} = x_{it-1} - \epsilon_{it}$

$$
\min_{\{x_{it}\}} \mathbb{E}_0 \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \beta^t
\left[
\frac{1}{2}\left(x_{it}-\widehat{MC}_t\right)^2
+
\xi \mathbf{1}_{\{x_{it} \ne x_{it-1}-\epsilon_{it}\}}
\right]
$$

- Lots of evidence for this kind of behavior in micro data on price setting!

## Slide 11: Mechanics of menu cost models

- Entire distribution of price gaps matters! “State dependent” model
- Hard to analyze… Main focus on permanent MC shocks, i.e. $\Psi \cdot \mathbf{1}$

## Slide 15: Numerical equivalence result

- Pass through matrix and generalized Phillips curve of menu cost models are well approximated by a Calvo model with a greater frequency of price resets.
- Since it holds for Jacobians, it holds for arbitrary menu cost shocks.
- This holds across various parameterizations of menu cost models
- To summarize, to first order:

Calvo

$$
\pi_t = \kappa \widehat{mc}_t + \beta \mathbb{E}_t \pi_{t+1}
$$

Menu cost

$$
\pi_t \approx \widetilde{\kappa}\widehat{mc}_t + \beta \mathbb{E}_t \pi_{t+1}
$$

$$
\widetilde{\kappa} > \kappa
$$

## Slide 16: More mechanics of menu cost model

Define persistence of price gap $x$:

$$
\rho^{(t)}(x) \equiv \frac{\mathbb{E}_0[x_t \mid x_0 = x]}{x}
$$

$$
\rho^{(t)}(0) \equiv \lim_{x \to 0} \rho^{(t)}(x)
$$

Image note: Diagram extends the menu cost mechanics picture with a black curve labeled “persistence $\rho^{(1)}(x)$.” It again shows “adjustment probability,” “steady state distribution,” “price gap,” and threshold labels $\underline{x}$, $0$, $x$, and $\bar{x}$.

## Slide 17: Exact equivalence result

- Menu cost model is exactly the same as mix of two time-dependent models
  - one with survival function equal to $\rho^{(t)}(0)$
  - one with survival function equal to $\rho^{(t)}(\bar{x})$
- Totally non-obvious!
- The two survival functions average to something close to exponential, ~ Calvo!
