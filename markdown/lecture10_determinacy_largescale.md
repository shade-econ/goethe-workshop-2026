## Slide 2: Roadmap for determinacy and existence

- Want a sequence-space criterion for determinacy and existence
- To get there: obtain a **structure theorem** for sequence-space Jacobians
- When het-agent model is stationary, Jacobians are quasi-Toeplitz:

$$
J = T(j) + E
$$

i.e. sum of Toeplitz operator $T(j)$ and compact operator $E$ on $\ell^2$

- Will exploit this structure in many ways, but start with:
  - “**Winding number**” criterion on $j$ for **determinacy & existence**

## Slide 3: Toeplitz and quasi-Toeplitz operators

## Slide 4: What is a Toeplitz operator?

- We’ll work with **semi-infinite** Toeplitz matrices $T(j)$ with constant diagonals $\{j_s\}_{s=-\infty}^{\infty}$.
- Assuming

$$
\sum_s |j_s| < \infty,
$$

these induce bounded operators on $\ell^2$.

- Can define series

$$
j(z) \equiv \sum_{s=-\infty}^{\infty} j_s z^s,
$$

sometimes called “**symbol**” of $T(j)$.

$$
T(j) =
\begin{pmatrix}
j_0 & j_{-1} & j_{-2} & \cdots \\
j_1 & j_0 & j_{-1} & \ddots \\
j_2 & j_1 & j_0 & \ddots \\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}
$$

## Slide 5: Examples: lag and lead operators

Lag operator $L$:

$$
L =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\
1 & 0 & 0 & \ddots \\
0 & 1 & 0 & \ddots \\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
L : (x_0, x_1, x_2, \ldots) \mapsto (0, x_0, x_1, \ldots)
$$

[Injective but not surjective (range missing 1 dimension)]

Lead operator $F$:

$$
F =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 1 & 0 & \cdots \\
0 & 0 & 1 & \ddots \\
0 & 0 & 0 & \ddots \\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
F : (x_0, x_1, x_2, \ldots) \mapsto (x_1, x_2, x_3, \ldots)
$$

[Surjective but not injective (null-space has 1 dimension)]

[= taking one-period-ahead expectations given MIT shock]

## Slide 6: More complex example: reset prices given marginal cost

- Log-linearizing standard Calvo model, get:

$$
p_t^* = (1 - \beta\theta)\cdot \sum_{s=0}^{\infty}(\beta\theta)^s \cdot \mathbb{E}_t MC_{t+s}
$$

- For MIT shock, mapping from $\{MC_t\}_{t=0}^{\infty}$ to $\{p_t^*\}_{t=0}^{\infty}$ is Toeplitz, equal to $(1-\beta\theta)$ times matrix on right:

[forward-looking $\rightarrow$ upper triangular]

$$
(1-\beta\theta)
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & \beta\theta & (\beta\theta)^2 & \cdots \\
0 & 1 & \beta\theta & \ddots \\
0 & 0 & 1 & \ddots \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}
$$

## Slide 10: What if we want to compose Toeplitz operators?

- Would be nice to deal only with Toeplitz operators
- **Problem:** class of Toeplitz operators not closed under composition / multiplication
- Simple example:

$$
FL = I
$$

(lead of lag is identity), but

$$
LF \ne I,
$$

and instead:

$$
LF =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\
0 & 1 & 0 & \ddots \\
0 & 0 & 1 & \ddots \\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}
$$

- So

$$
(LF)\cdot (x_0,x_1,x_2,\ldots) = (0,x_1,x_2,\ldots)
$$

- Interpretation for MIT shocks? “**Missing anticipation.**”
  - $\mathbb{E}_{t-1}x_t = x_t$ for $t>0$, but $\mathbb{E}_{-1}x_0 = 0 \ne x_0$

## Slide 11: But we do stay in larger quasi-Toeplitz class

- Define **quasi-Toeplitz** operators as Toeplitz operator plus compact operator $E$.

[compact in $\ell^2$ = can be uniformly well-approximated by finite rank]

$$
J = T(j) + E
$$

- **Result:** product of any two Toeplitz operators $T(j_1)$ and $T(j_2)$ is quasi-Toeplitz like above, with

$$
j = j_1 \cdot j_2
$$

given by convolution

[so $j(z)=j_1(z)j_2(z)$]

- Intuitively, why do we need $E$? “Missing anticipation”: taking lags of leads!
- Quasi-Toeplitz operators closed under multiplication!

[multiplying Toeplitz parts $\rightarrow$ quasi-Toeplitz, multiplying any bounded by compact $\rightarrow$ compact]

## Slide 12: Compose to get map $J$ from costs to aggregate prices

$$
(1-\theta)
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\
\theta & 1 & 0 & \ddots \\
\theta^2 & \theta & 1 & \ddots \\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}
\times
(1-\beta\theta)
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & \beta\theta & (\beta\theta)^2 & \cdots \\
0 & 1 & \beta\theta & \ddots \\
0 & 0 & 1 & \ddots \\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix}
$$

(Lag of lead: we should expect a compact “correction” $E$!)

## Slide 14: Structure theorem for heterogeneous-agent models

## Slide 15: Structure theorem for heterogeneous-agent models

- Such models have some steady-state transition matrix $\Lambda_{ss}$, some backward mapping $v_{t+1}\to v_t$ on value function, with steady-state derivative $v_v$.
- Suppose we have “**stationarity**”:
  - $\Lambda_{ss}$ has all eigenvalues but one strictly inside unit circle
  - $v_v$ has eigenvalues strictly inside unit circle
- **Structure theorem:** if model is stationary, all sequence-space Jacobians are quasi-Toeplitz with

$$
|E_{ts}| \le K\Delta^{t+s}
$$

for bound $\Delta$ on eigenvalues and some constant $K$.

- Intuition: if effect of far future on value function eventually dies off...
  - ... and effect of distribution on future eventually dies off, “missing anticipation” dies off too

## Slide 19: Determinacy, existence, and inversion

## Slide 20: Winding number of a Toeplitz operator

- **Winding number** $\operatorname{wind}(j)$ is # of times symbol $j(z)$ rotates counterclockwise around 0 as $z$ goes counterclockwise around the unit circle.

[see Onatski 2006]

- **Result:** can invert Toeplitz $T(j)$, resulting in quasi-Toeplitz, iff winding number 0.
- For quasi-Toeplitz $T(j)+E$, this result is “generic” on open & dense set of $E$:
  - If $\operatorname{wind}(j)=0$, $J$ is generically **invertible**
  - If $\operatorname{wind}(j)<0$, $J$ is **not injective (indeterminacy)**, but generically surjective
  - If $\operatorname{wind}(j)>0$, $J$ is **not surjective (nonexistence)**, but generically injective
- [Example: lag operator has $j(z)=z$, winding number of 1, so not surjective.]

## Slide 21: Application: uniqueness in Intertemporal Keynesian Cross model

- In Intertemporal Keynesian Cross model (Auclert Rognlie Straub 2024), equilibrium output $dY$ given taxes $dT$ and bonds $dB$ given by

$$
A(dY-dT)=dB
$$

where $A$ is Jacobian of household assets to post-tax income.

- $A$ is quasi-Toeplitz, will generically be invertible with solution

$$
dY=A^{-1}dB+dT
$$

if winding number of $a(z)$ is zero.

- Multiple equilibria $dY$ if winding number is negative
  - (Self-fulfilling booms or busts in output!)

## Slide 26: Under the hood: winding number code

Once you have $j(z)$, it’s easy to evaluate at sample points around unit circle very efficiently with the FFT—see left.

(**Much better** than a naive implementation.)

Then we just need to count the number of times the path wraps around the origin—tedious function to write, header below, full function in `winding_number.py`.

```python
def sample_values_simple(j, K=8192):
    """Evaluate Laurent polynomial j(z) (with equally many
    positive and negative powers) counterclockwise at K evenly
    spaced roots of unity z, wrapping back around to z=1"""
    # j is (2*T-1,) array of Laurent coeffs (powers from -(T-1) to T-1)
    assert (K % 2 == 0) and (len(j) % 2 == 1) and (K > len(j))
    T = len(j) // 2 + 1

    # center j(z) at K/2, fill rest with zeros
    jhat = np.zeros(K)
    jhat[(K//2 - (T - 1)):(K//2 + T)] = j

    # take FFT of jhat to evaluate z^(K/2)*j(z) at clockwise roots of unity
    x = np.fft.fft(jhat)

    # divide by z^(K/2), which is alternating 1, -1 at roots of unity
    alt = np.tile([1, -1], K//2)
    x *= alt

    # return wrapped back to z=1, reversed to make counterclockwise
    return np.concatenate((x, [x[0]]))[::-1]
```

```python
@njit
def winding_number_of_path(x, y):
    """Compute winding number around origin of (x,y) coordinates that make
    closed path by
    counting number of counterclockwise crossings of ray from (0,0) ->
    (infty,0) on x axis"""
```

## Slide 27: Block quasi-Toeplitz case

- Say we have $N^2$ quasi-Toeplitz matrices from $N$ unknowns to one of $N$ targets.
- Can think of this as being one **block quasi-Toeplitz** operator, like a quasi-Toeplitz but where entries are each $N\times N$ blocks.
- Then $\{j_k\}_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}$ is two-sided sequence of $N\times N$ matrices, so matrix-valued $j(z)$:

$$
j(z)\equiv \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} j_k z^k
$$

- Winding number test still holds generically, now for $\operatorname{wind}(\det j)$.
- Important case in practice.

## Slide 31: Operations with quasi-Toeplitz operators: No more truncation!

## Slide 32: Directly use quasi-Toeplitz form

- We have quasi-Toeplitz representation

$$
J = T(j) + E
$$

of Jacobians.

- So far, we’ve used the winding number of $j$ to assess determinacy.
- Going beyond this: directly do computations with this representation!
  - Benefit: $E$ decays quickly to zero, and often close to low-rank

$$
E \approx UV^\prime
$$

  - Cheap to get $T(j_1j_2)$, multiply $T(j_1)U_2^\prime$, etc., to construct $J_1J_2$ (using FFT)
  - Similar, though a bit more complex, for inversion
- Working directly with quasi-Toeplitz (infinite!), not truncated matrices, avoids errors from truncation.

[Bini, Massei, Robol 2019]

## Slide 35: Alternative: use structure for iterative solutions (and solve giant models in the process!)

## Slide 36: First point: easy to get Toeplitz part of inverse

- Suppose we want to solve

$$
A\,dZ = dB
$$

- $A^{-1}$ is quasi-Toeplitz of form $T(a^{-1})+E$, with $E$ low-rank like we saw.
- Key point: $a^{-1}$ is really easy to calculate!
  - Get $a(z)$ at many $z$ using FFT, then go from $a(z)^{-1}$ to $a^{-1}$ with inverse FFT
  - Cost is only $O(T\log T)$, way cheaper than $O(T^3)$ matrix inversion
- What can we do with just $T(a^{-1})$?
  - [conceptually, $a^{-1}$ is inverse for infinitely-well-anticipated shocks]

## Slide 37: What can we do with $a^{-1}$?

- Start with $(T(a)+E)dZ=dB$, multiply both sides by $T(a^{-1})$:

$$
T(a^{-1})(T(a)+E)dZ = T(a^{-1})dB
$$

- Both $T(a^{-1})T(a)-I$ and $T(a^{-1})E$ compact, well-approximated by low rank, so can be written in form

$$
(I+C)dZ=dy
$$

- Iterative method (GMRES) very good at solving $(I+C)^{-1}dy$ if $C$ low-rank.

[Multiplying by $T(a^{-1})$ is called “preconditioning”.]

- Cheap, doesn’t require explicitly forming new matrices like $C$.
